<Intuition>
The course teaches every method; the practice bank is where you make them automatic. It
holds over **11,000 questions** — more than you will need — so you never run out, never
memorize a repeated question, and can always find more practice on exactly the skill you
are weakest in. Every question has an explanation, and every multiple-choice option has a
note saying why it is right or why it tempts people.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## What’s inside

**Quantitative (about 6,750 questions):** every quant topic in the course — number
properties, exponents, fractions, percents, ratios, rates, algebra, geometry, statistics,
counting and probability, data interpretation tables — plus three dedicated families of
quantitative comparison questions. All four quant formats appear: quantitative comparison,
multiple choice, select-one-or-more, and numeric entry (including fraction entry).

**Verbal (about 5,000 questions):** text completion with one, two, and three blanks;
sentence equivalence; reading comprehension passages; argument questions; and four kinds of
vocabulary drill built on the core word families.

The quant questions are generated from templates with carefully chosen numbers, and every
one is automatically checked for a valid answer and distinct choices. Verbal questions are
built from the course’s word families and hand-written sentences, passages, and arguments.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Four ways to practice

1. **Numbered sets** (the default path). Each track — quant and verbal — is divided into
   sets of about 20 questions. Every set mixes all topics at about the same difficulty, and
   the sets ramp through three tiers: **Foundation → Core → Advanced**. The next set is
   always highlighted; your score on every finished set is shown on its tile.
2. **Timed sections.** Any set can be run timed, at real GRE pace, with feedback held until
   the end — exactly like the test. Your results page lists every miss with a full review.
3. **Topic drills.** Pick one topic (for example, “Successive percent changes” or
   “Sentence equivalence”) and get ten questions you haven’t seen yet, easiest first.
4. **Mistakes.** Every missed question stays on your mistakes list until you answer it
   correctly. Misses also go into the review queue, which brings them back after a day and
   then at growing intervals. For most quant topics, review shows a fresh variant with new
   numbers.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## A routine that works

- **Daily:** your review queue first (usually 5–15 minutes), then the day’s sets from the
  study plan.
- **After each set:** open the “By topic” breakdown. Any topic below about 70% gets a topic
  drill the next day, and a reread of its lesson if the drill goes badly too.
- **Weekly:** one or two timed sets per track, and compare your pacing with the checkpoints
  from the test-day lesson.
- **When a tier feels easy** (consistently 85%+), move up to the next tier rather than
  finishing every set in it.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Beyond the bank: five full-length practice tests

The bank builds skills one set at a time; the **full-length practice tests** (course
dashboard → Full-length tests) check them all at once, under test conditions. Each test runs
like the real GRE:

- **Analytical Writing first:** one Issue essay, 30 minutes, no spell-check.
- **Then four scored sections** — two Verbal (12 questions in 18 minutes, then 15 in 23)
  and two Quant (12 in 21, then 15 in 26). You can move back and forth, **mark** questions,
  and use the **review** screen within a section; once a section ends, it’s closed.
- **The second section adapts:** 7 or more right in a measure’s first section routes you to
  its harder second section; fewer routes you to the easier one. As on the real test, the
  harder route unlocks the top of the 130–170 scale.
- **Results:** estimated Verbal and Quant scores (as a ±3 band — ETS doesn’t publish its
  scoring formula), time used per section, breakdowns by question type and topic, an
  explanation for every question, and one click to send your misses to the review queue.
  The tutor can also critique your essay against the scoring rubric.

The questions are original, written to match the real test’s formats, topics, difficulty,
and traps. Take one in the weeks the study plan names, and add the free official POWERPREP
tests from ETS in the final two weeks.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — counting questions instead of learning from them">
Doing 200 questions and reading none of the explanations teaches less than doing 40 and
studying every miss. The value is in the notes under each choice: they name the trap you
fell for, so you can stop falling for it.
</Gotcha>
