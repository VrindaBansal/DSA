<Intuition>
GRE geometry uses a small toolkit over and over: angles on a line add to 180°, a triangle’s
angles add to 180°, and right triangles follow the Pythagorean theorem and a few special
ratios. Most questions combine two of those facts. One rule overrides everything:
**figures are not necessarily drawn to scale.** Use what is stated, not what looks true.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Angles

- Angles on a straight line add to **180°**; around a point, **360°**.
- Vertical angles (opposite each other where two lines cross) are **equal**.
- A transversal across **parallel** lines creates only two angle measures: some angle
  x and its supplement 180° − x.
- A triangle’s angles add to **180°**. An **exterior angle** equals the sum of the two
  remote interior angles.
- In an **isosceles** triangle, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal. In an
  equilateral triangle, all three angles are 60°.
- The largest angle is opposite the longest side.
</Concept>

<Example title="Angles in a ratio">
The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 7. What is the largest angle?

<Solution>
2 + 3 + 7 = 12 parts = 180°, so one part = 15°. Largest = 7 × 15° = **105°**.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Right triangles

**Pythagorean theorem:** in a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c,
a² + b² = c². Memorize the triples — they save the arithmetic.
</Concept>

- **45-45-90:** sides x : x : x√2. It is half of a square, cut along the diagonal.
- **30-60-90:** sides x : x√3 : 2x (short leg opposite 30°, hypotenuse = twice the short
  leg). It is half of an equilateral triangle, so an equilateral triangle with side s has
  height (s/2)√3 and area s²√3/4.
</Concept>

<Example title="A special right triangle">
The hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 triangle is 10. What is its area?

<Solution>
Short leg = 10 ÷ 2 = 5. Long leg = 5√3. Area = ½ × 5 × 5√3 = **(25√3)/2** ≈ 21.65.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## The triangle inequality

Any side is **less than the sum** and **greater than the difference** of the other two.
With sides 4 and 9, the third side x satisfies 5 < x < 13.
</Concept>

<Check id="gre-tri-ineq" />

<Concept>
## Similar figures

Similar figures have the same shape. If corresponding sides are in ratio k, then
**perimeters** are in ratio k, **areas** in ratio k², and **volumes** in ratio k³.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — trusting the figure">
A triangle that looks like a right triangle, or two lines that look parallel, are not
known to be unless the question says so (or marks the right angle). On quantitative
comparison, that uncertainty often makes the answer D.
</Gotcha>
