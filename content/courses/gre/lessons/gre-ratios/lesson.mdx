<Intuition>
A ratio tells you the **shape** of a group, not its size. “Cats to dogs is 3 : 5” means
the group can be cut into 8 equal parts: 3 are cats, 5 are dogs. Once you know the size of
one part, everything else follows. Nearly every ratio problem is “find one part” in
disguise.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Parts

With a ratio a : b, the whole has a + b parts. If you know the total, one part = total ÷
(a + b). If you know the difference, one part = difference ÷ (a − b).
</Concept>

<Example title="Ratio to count">
The ratio of fiction to nonfiction books on a shelf is 4 : 7. There are 66 books. How many
are nonfiction?

<Solution>
4 + 7 = 11 parts; one part = 66 ÷ 11 = 6. Nonfiction = 7 × 6 = **42**.

Trap: 66 × 7/4 treats the ratio as a fraction of the total. The total has 11 parts, not 4.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Combining ratios

If a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, make b the same in both. b is 3 in one and 4 in the
other, so scale to 12: a : b = 8 : 12 and b : c = 12 : 15. Then a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15, and
a : c = **8 : 15**.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Ratios that change: find what stays fixed

When something is added to only one part of a ratio, the other part is fixed. Write the
original amounts as multiples of one unknown (3k and 5k), apply the change, and set up the
new ratio.
</Concept>

<Example title="A changing ratio">
The ratio of boys to girls in a club is 3 : 4. After 6 more boys join, the ratio is 1 : 1.
How many girls are in the club?

<Solution>
Boys = 3k, girls = 4k. After: (3k + 6) : 4k = 1 : 1 → 3k + 6 = 4k → k = 6.
Girls = 4 × 6 = **24** (and boys go from 18 to 24 ✓).
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Proportional scaling

If 4 machines make 60 widgets in 3 hours, one machine makes 60 ÷ (4 × 3) = 5 widgets per
hour. Then any question is just 5 × machines × hours. More machines → more output; more
time → more output.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — scaling the wrong way">
When asked how LONG a job takes with more workers, time goes DOWN (inverse), while output
in a fixed time goes UP (direct). Sanity-check the direction before you compute.
</Gotcha>
