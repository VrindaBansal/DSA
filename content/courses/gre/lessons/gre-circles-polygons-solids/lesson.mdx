<Intuition>
Everything about a circle comes from its radius. Everything about an arc or sector is a
fraction of the whole circle — the central angle over 360°. Polygons reduce to triangles,
and solids reduce to “area of the base times height.” Learn those four ideas and most of
this topic is arithmetic.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Circles

- Diameter d = 2r. Circumference C = 2πr = πd. Area A = πr².
- **Arc length** = (θ/360) × 2πr and **sector area** = (θ/360) × πr², where θ is the
  central angle.
- An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle. A tangent is perpendicular to the
  radius at the point of contact.
- Answers are usually left in terms of π: “36π,” not 113.1.
</Concept>

<Example title="A sector">
A circle has area 64π. What is the area of a sector with a 45° central angle?

<Solution>
πr² = 64π → r = 8 (you don’t even need r). 45° is 45/360 = ⅛ of the circle.
Sector area = ⅛ × 64π = **8π**.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Polygons

- Sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = **(n − 2) × 180°** (it splits into n − 2
  triangles).
- Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n. Each **exterior** angle
  = 360° ÷ n — often the faster route.
- Rectangle: area = lw; diagonal = √(l² + w²). Square: area = s² = diagonal² ÷ 2.
- Parallelogram: area = base × height (the perpendicular height, not the slanted side).
  Trapezoid: area = ½(b₁ + b₂)h.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Solids

- Rectangular box: V = lwh; surface area = 2(lw + lh + wh); longest diagonal
  = √(l² + w² + h²).
- Cube: V = s³; surface area = 6s².
- Cylinder: V = πr²h (area of the base × height); curved surface area = 2πrh.

**Scaling:** multiply every length by k and volume multiplies by k³. Double a cylinder’s
radius only and its volume multiplies by 4.
</Concept>

<Example title="Box diagonal">
What is the length of the longest segment that fits inside a 3 × 4 × 12 box?

<Solution>
√(3² + 4² + 12²) = √(9 + 16 + 144) = √169 = **13**.

(It is two Pythagorean steps: the base diagonal is 5, then √(5² + 12²) = 13.)
</Solution>
</Example>

<Gotcha title="Trap — radius or diameter?">
Circle questions often give the diameter while the formulas use the radius. Before you
square anything, write down r.
</Gotcha>
