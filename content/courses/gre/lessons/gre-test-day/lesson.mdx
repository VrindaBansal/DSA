<Intuition>
By test day, your score is mostly decided by what you've learned. What's left is **not losing points you
have already earned**. That means:

- pacing so you **see every question**,
- **guessing** where you must,
- staying **calm** when a section feels hard, and
- arriving **rested**.

None of this is glamorous, and all of it is worth points.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. Two passes through every section

**Pass 1:** answer everything you can do in about 1½–2 minutes. When a question will take
longer or you have no plan, click your best guess, **mark it**, and move on.

**Pass 2:** use the remaining time on marked questions, easiest first. Change answers only
when you find a concrete error — not on a hunch.

Before time runs out, make sure **every** question has an answer. There is no penalty for
wrong answers.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## 2. Pacing checkpoints

Glance at the clock at the halfway point of each section. If you're behind, switch to pass-1 mode right
away: answer quick questions, guess-and-mark the rest.

| Section | Questions | Time | Halfway checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal 1 | 12 | 18 min | question 6 by about 9 minutes |
| Verbal 2 | 15 | 23 min | question 8 by about 12 minutes |
| Quant 1 | 12 | 21 min | question 6 by about 10–11 minutes |
| Quant 2 | 15 | 26 min | question 8 by about 13 minutes |

In Verbal, reading passages take longer than text completions, so it's normal to be a little ahead at
the checkpoint when the first questions were completions.
</Concept>

<Example title="Are you on pace?">
You're in the first Quant section (12 questions, 21 minutes). The clock shows 12 minutes used, and
you've just finished question 5. Are you on pace, and what should you do?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Find the checkpoint.** Quant 1's halfway point is question 6 by about 10–11 minutes.

**Step 2 — Compare.** You've used 12 minutes and finished only 5 — about two minutes behind.

**Step 3 — Work out what's left.** 21 − 12 = 9 minutes for 7 questions: about 1.3 minutes each,
faster than the average of 1.75.

**Step 4 — Act.** Switch to pass-1 mode now: answer the quick questions, guess and mark anything that
isn't quick, and come back to marked ones if time remains.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 3. Staying steady

- **The first section of each subject matters most** (it sets the second section’s
  difficulty). Give it full focus.
- **A hard second section is usually good news.** It means you did well on the first.
- **One bad question is one question.** Guess, mark, move. The next one counts the same.
- Use the scratch paper: write A B C D E for each question and cross out eliminated
  choices, so a second look takes seconds.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## 4. Logistics checklist

- Check the current ETS requirements for ID, arrival time, and (for the at-home test) your
  computer, camera, room, and whiteboard or transparent sheet — rules change, so read them
  on the official site a week before.
- Know how you will get to the test center, or test your at-home setup in advance.
- Eat beforehand and have water ready: the shorter GRE runs about two hours without a
  scheduled break.
- Decide beforehand which programs will receive your scores.
- You will see your unofficial Verbal and Quant scores at the end of the test; the official
  report, including the Writing score, follows later.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## 5. The final week

- Take your last full practice test — Practice Test 5 is built as the dress rehearsal, or an
  official POWERPREP test — **no later than 3–4 days** before the test.
- Last two days: review cheatsheets and your review queue, do a small timed set to stay
  sharp, and stop early. Cramming new material now costs more than it adds.
- Sleep. A rested brain reads more carefully, and careful reading is half of Verbal.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — carrying a bad section into the next one">
If a section felt bad, you cannot know how it actually went — and it’s over. When the next
section starts, take one slow breath and treat it as a fresh start.
</Gotcha>
