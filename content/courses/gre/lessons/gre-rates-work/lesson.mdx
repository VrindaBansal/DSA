<Intuition>
Rate problems have one engine: **amount = rate × time**. Distance, pages printed, a tank
filled, a fence painted — all the same equation. The three traps are also the same every
time: averaging speeds that were held for different amounts of time, adding times when you
should add rates, and averaging concentrations when the volumes differ. In each case the fix
is to go back to the totals.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Average speed is total distance ÷ total time

It is **not** the average of the speeds unless equal *times* were spent at each speed.
</Concept>

<Example title="The round trip">
You drive to a lake at 30 mph and back along the same road at 60 mph. What is your average
speed for the round trip?

<Solution>
Pick a distance that divides easily: 60 miles each way.
Out: 60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours. Back: 60 ÷ 60 = 1 hour. Total: 120 miles in 3 hours → **40 mph**.

Not 45: you spent twice as long at the slower speed, so it counts more. (For equal
distances, average speed = 2uv/(u + v).)
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Two movers

- **Toward each other:** the gap closes at the **sum** of the speeds.
- **Same direction (catching up):** the gap closes at the **difference** of the speeds.

Two cars 300 miles apart driving toward each other at 40 and 60 mph meet after
300 ÷ 100 = 3 hours.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Work: add rates, never times

If A finishes a job in a hours, A does 1/a of the job per hour. Working together, rates add:
1/a + 1/b. Time together = 1 ÷ (1/a + 1/b) = ab/(a + b).
</Concept>

<Example title="Working together">
One painter can paint a room in 3 hours; another takes 6 hours. How long if they work
together?

<Solution>
Rates: 1/3 + 1/6 = 2/6 + 1/6 = 1/2 room per hour → **2 hours**.

Sanity check: together must be faster than the faster painter alone (3 hours). Averaging
the times (4.5 hours) fails that check immediately.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Check id="gre-rw-work" />

<Concept>
## Mixtures: track the pure stuff

Concentration = amount of substance ÷ total volume. Mixing adds **amounts** and adds
**volumes**; it never adds or averages concentrations directly (unless the volumes are
equal).

3 L of 20% salt solution + 1 L of 60% salt solution:
salt = 0.6 + 0.6 = 1.2 L; volume = 4 L → **30%**.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — adding pure water">
Adding water changes the volume but not the amount of substance. To dilute 10 L of a 40%
solution to 25%, keep the 4 L of substance fixed: 4 = 0.25 × (10 + w) → w = 6 L of water.
</Gotcha>
