<Intuition>
Many GRE math questions are built so that **solving** them with algebra is slow, but **checking** an
answer is fast. When that's the case, stop solving and start checking:

- **Plug in** your own numbers for the variables,
- **backsolve** by testing the answer choices, or
- **estimate** when the choices are far apart.

These aren't tricks for people who can't do algebra. Strong test-takers use them all the time to save
minutes for the hardest questions.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. Plug in numbers

**Use it when the answer choices contain variables** (like "np/100" or "60h/m").

1. **Pick easy numbers** for each variable. Avoid 0 and 1 (they make too many expressions equal) and
   avoid using the same number twice.
2. **Answer the question** with your numbers. This gives you a **target** number.
3. **Plug the same numbers into each choice.** The one that hits the target is the answer.
4. If two choices both hit, try new numbers on just those two.

**For percent problems, plug in 100.** It makes every percent easy to see.
</Concept>

<Example title="Plugging in">
A store sells pens for p cents each. How many **dollars** do n pens cost?

(A) np  (B) 100np  (C) np/100  (D) 100n/p  (E) p/(100n)

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Pick numbers.** p = 50 cents and n = 4 pens.

**Step 2 — Find the target.** 4 pens × 50¢ = 200¢ = **$2**.

**Step 3 — Test each choice with p = 50, n = 4.**
- (A) 4 × 50 = 200 ✗
- (B) 100 × 4 × 50 = 20,000 ✗
- (C) 200 ÷ 100 = **2** ✓
- (D) 400 ÷ 50 = 8 ✗
- (E) 50 ÷ 400 = 0.125 ✗

**Answer: (C) np/100.** No reasoning about unit conversions in the abstract.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 2. Backsolve — work backward from the choices

**Use it when the choices are numbers and the question asks for a single value.**

The answer is one of the five choices, so just **try them** in the problem's story. Choices are almost
always listed from smallest to largest, so:

1. **Start with the middle choice (C).**
2. If it works, you're done.
3. If it gives a result that's **too big**, the answer is smaller — try (A) or (B). **Too small**, try
   (D) or (E).

At most you'll test two or three choices, and each test is just arithmetic.
</Concept>

<Example title="Backsolving">
A number is tripled, then 12 is subtracted. The result is 4 more than the original number. What is the
number?

(A) 4  (B) 6  (C) 8  (D) 10  (E) 12

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Try the middle choice, 8.** Tripled: 24. Minus 12: 12.

**Step 2 — Check the condition.** Is 12 equal to 8 + 4? Yes ✓.

**Answer: (C) 8.**

**If it had failed:** say (C) gave a result bigger than it should be — then you'd know to try a smaller
choice next. The size of the miss tells you which way to go.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 3. Estimate — and use the calculator wisely

**Look at the answer choices before you calculate.** If they're far apart, round the numbers and pick
the closest choice.

- 30.2% of 596 ≈ 30% of 600 = **180**.
- (8.1 × 49.7) ÷ 2.02 ≈ (8 × 50) ÷ 2 = **200**.

If the choices are **close together**, estimating isn't safe — do the exact math.

**The on-screen calculator** is slow (you click every button) and it follows the order of operations.
Use it for ugly arithmetic you've already set up, like 1,284 × 0.37 — not for things faster in your
head, like 20% of 400. For numeric-entry questions, its **Transfer Display** button copies the result
into the answer box.
</Concept>

<Example title="Estimate first">
Which of the following is closest to (61.8 × 9.93) ÷ 2.97?

(A) 20  (B) 200  (C) 2,000  (D) 20,000  (E) 200,000

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Round every number.** 61.8 ≈ 60, 9.93 ≈ 10, 2.97 ≈ 3.

**Step 2 — Compute the easy version.** (60 × 10) ÷ 3 = 600 ÷ 3 = **200**.

**Step 3 — Check that rounding is safe.** The choices are 10 times apart, so a small rounding error
can't change the answer. **(B) 200.** (Exact: about 206.6.)
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 4. The special question formats

**Select one or more answer choices** (square boxes instead of circles):
- The question doesn't tell you how many are correct — it could be one, some, or all.
- There's **no partial credit**: you must pick exactly the right set.
- **Find the rule** instead of testing each choice blindly. If the question describes a range of
  values, solve for that range once, then just read off which choices are inside it.

**Numeric entry** (you type the answer):
- Enter the **exact** value unless the question says to round.
- Fractions go in a two-box format; equivalent fractions (like 6/8 for 3/4) are accepted.
- **Check the units** the question asks for. A question asking for minutes wants minutes, not hours.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## 5. Answer the right question, and pace yourself

**Wrong answer choices are often right answers to a different question**: the width when the question
asked for the length, x when it asked for x + 2, the number of red marbles when it asked for blue.
Before you click, **reread the last line of the question.**

**Pacing.** Each Quant section gives you a little under 2 minutes per question, and every question is
worth the same. So:
- If you've spent about 2½ minutes and still don't have a plan, **guess, mark it, and move on.**
- **Never leave a question blank.** There's no penalty for a wrong answer.
- Use the **Review** screen at the end of a section to return to the questions you marked.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — the answer to a different question">
In a problem about a rectangle's perimeter, the choices will usually include the width, the length, and
their sum. You found the width — great, but you're not done. Circle the final question on your scratch
paper so you don't stop early.
</Gotcha>

<Concept>
## Recap

| Tactic | Use it when | Key move |
|---|---|---|
| plug in | variables in the answer choices | pick easy numbers, find a target, test every choice |
| plug in 100 | percent problems with no starting value | start at 100 |
| backsolve | numeric choices, "what is x?" | start with the middle choice |
| estimate | choices far apart | round first, then compute |
| select-all | square boxes | find the rule once; no partial credit |
| numeric entry | a box to type in | exact value, right units |
| reread | before every click | answer the question asked |
| pace | stuck around 2½ min | guess, mark, move on — never leave a blank |
</Concept>

<Cheatsheet>
Use these tactics throughout the practice bank; timed sections will show you where you spend too long.
</Cheatsheet>
