<Intuition>
Function questions test **careful substitution**, not advanced theory. f(x) is a machine:
put a number in wherever you see x. The GRE’s made-up symbols (“a ◆ b = 2a − b”) are the
same machine with two inputs. And sequences are patterns you can almost always just write
out. The way to lose points here is rushing — so the habit to build is parentheses around
every substituted value.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Substitution with parentheses

If f(x) = x² − 3x, then f(−2) = (−2)² − 3(−2) = 4 + 6 = 10. Without parentheses it is easy
to write −2² = −4 or −3 × −2 = −6.

**Composition** f(g(x)): work from the inside out. Compute g(x) first, then feed the result
into f. Usually f(g(x)) ≠ g(f(x)).
</Concept>

<Example title="Composition">
f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x² − 3. Find f(g(2)) and g(f(2)).

<Solution>
f(g(2)): g(2) = 4 − 3 = 1, then f(1) = 3 → **3**.
g(f(2)): f(2) = 5, then g(5) = 25 − 3 = 22 → **22**.

Same functions, different order, different answers.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Defined symbols

“For all numbers a and b, a ◆ b = a² − ab.” Just substitute: 3 ◆ 5 = 9 − 15 = −6. Keep the
order (a first, b second), and do the parentheses first in nested expressions like
(1 ◆ 2) ◆ 3.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Sequences

- **Arithmetic** (add d each time): aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d. Sum of n terms = n × (first + last)/2.
- **Geometric** (multiply by r each time): aₙ = a₁ × r^(n − 1).
- **Recursive** (“aₙ = 2aₙ₋₁ + 1”): write the terms out; the GRE keeps these short.

The (n − 1) is where most errors happen: to get from term 1 to term 10 you take 9 steps.
</Concept>

<Example title="Two known terms">
In an arithmetic sequence, the 4th term is 17 and the 9th term is 42. What is the 20th term?

<Solution>
From term 4 to term 9 is 5 steps and the value rises 25, so d = 5.
From term 9 to term 20 is 11 more steps: 42 + 11 × 5 = **97**.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Variation

- **Direct:** y = kx. y/x stays constant. Double x → double y.
- **Inverse:** y = k/x. xy stays constant. Double x → halve y.

Find k from the given pair, then use it. If y varies inversely as x and y = 12 when x = 3,
then k = 36 and y = 4 when x = 9.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — “varies with the square”">
“y varies directly as the square of x” means y = kx². Doubling x multiplies y by 4, not 2.
</Gotcha>
