<Intuition>
You learn this material the way you learn anything you want to keep: **see an example,
try one yourself, get told exactly why you were right or wrong, then meet the idea again
a few days later.** That loop is built into every part of this course. Your job is
simply to show up for it daily. The plan below tells you what to do each week so you
never have to decide — you just open the next thing.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## The four parts of the course, and what each is for

**Lessons** teach one skill each. Every lesson has worked examples — try each one before
you reveal the solution; the struggle is where the learning happens. Then **checks**
confirm you have it: every answer choice comes with a note explaining why it is right or
tempting-but-wrong. Read the notes even when you get the question right.

**The practice bank** holds over 11,000 questions, dealt into numbered sets of about 20.
Each set mixes every topic at the same difficulty, and the sets ramp through three tiers:
Foundation, Core, and Advanced. You can also drill a single topic or rerun your mistakes.

**The review queue** brings back every question you miss — a day later, then at growing
intervals as you keep getting it right. For quant, it usually serves a *fresh variant*
(same skill, new numbers), so you practice the method rather than remembering one answer.
This is your error log, maintained for you.

**The tutor** (bottom right, or ⌘K) answers questions about any lesson. Select any text in
a lesson and click “explain this” to ask about exactly that sentence.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## The 8-week plan

Plan on **about 1½–2 hours a day, six days a week.** Lessons follow the course order,
which alternates quant and verbal so neither goes cold. “1 + 1 sets” means one quant set
and one verbal set in the practice bank, in order, in practice mode (feedback after each
question).
</Concept>

and 8, under real timing, in one sitting.

<Example title="Planning around a shorter runway">
Your test is in **4 weeks**, not 8, and you can study about 2½ hours a day. How would you
compress the plan?

<Solution>
Keep the order, double the pace:

- **Weeks 1–2:** two to three lessons a day (the whole course is 29 lessons of roughly
  20–35 minutes each), plus 1 + 1 Foundation sets daily.
- **Week 3:** Core-tier sets, one timed per track daily; drill your three weakest topics.
- **Week 4:** Advanced-tier timed sets, one official practice test early in the week, and
  your review queue every day. Rest the day before the test.

What you **don’t** cut: the review queue and timed practice. Those turn “I understood the
lesson” into “I get it right under time pressure,” which is what the test measures.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## How to review a missed question

When you miss something, don’t just read the right answer and move on. Ask:

1. **Which choice did I pick, and why was it tempting?** The notes under each choice tell
   you which trap you fell for. Traps repeat — learn their names.
2. **Was it knowledge, method, or carelessness?** Knowledge → reread the lesson (every
   bank question links to the lesson that teaches it). Method → redo a few questions of that
   type from the topic drills. Carelessness → slow down on that step next time.
3. **Can I solve it again cold, tomorrow?** The review queue will check.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — practicing without timing">
Untimed practice teaches methods; only timed practice teaches pacing. Many people who
“know everything” lose points because they never practiced deciding when to move on.
From week 4, at least one set a day should be timed.
</Gotcha>
