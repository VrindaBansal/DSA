<Intuition>
You learn this material the way you learn anything you want to keep:

1. **see a worked example,**
2. **try one yourself,**
3. **find out exactly why you were right or wrong,** and
4. **meet the idea again a few days later.**

That loop is built into every part of this course. Your job is simply to show up for it daily. The plan
below tells you what to do each week, so you never have to decide — you just open the next thing.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. The parts of the course, and what each is for

**Lessons** teach one topic each, one idea at a time. For every idea you get a plain-language
explanation, a **worked example** with every step shown, and then one or two **GRE-style practice
questions** on that idea. Try each worked example before you reveal the solution — the struggle is where
the learning happens. Every practice question explains the answer step by step, and every answer choice
has a note saying why it's right or tempting-but-wrong. Read the notes even when you get the question
right.

**The practice bank** holds over 11,000 questions, dealt into numbered sets of about 20.
Each set mixes every topic at the same difficulty, and the sets ramp through three tiers:
Foundation, Core, and Advanced. You can also drill a single topic or rerun your mistakes.

**Five timed full-length practice tests** run exactly like the real exam: the Issue essay, then
two Verbal and two Quant sections on the official clock, with a second section that gets
harder or easier depending on how you did on the first. At the end you get estimated
130–170 scores, a breakdown by topic, and an explanation for every question. They tell you
where you stand and build the stamina to stay sharp for two hours. **Five more — Practice
Tests 6–10 — are untimed and skip the essay:** the same four adaptive sections and the same
scoring, but no clock, so you can work every question by the method and see what your
accuracy is when time isn’t a factor.

**The review queue** brings back every question you miss — a day later, then at growing
intervals as you keep getting it right. For quant, it usually serves a *fresh variant*
(same skill, new numbers), so you practice the method rather than remembering one answer.
This is your error log, maintained for you.

**The tutor** (bottom right, or ⌘K) answers questions about any lesson. Select any text in
a lesson and click “explain this” to ask about exactly that sentence.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## 2. The 8-week plan

Plan on **about 1½–2 hours a day, six days a week.** Lessons take 35–55 minutes each; on a day with a
lesson, do the lesson first and the practice sets after. The course has two sections —
**Math** (Quantitative Reasoning, 6 modules) and **English** (Verbal Reasoning and the
essay, 5 modules) — plus *Getting started* and *Test day*. Each section runs in order on
its own, and the dashboard shows the next lesson in each. Work on **both every week**, as in
the table below, so neither goes cold. “1 + 1 sets” means one quant set and one verbal set
in the practice bank, in order, in practice mode (feedback after each question).
</Concept>

**Full-length tests.** Take every timed practice test (1–5) in one sitting, under real
timing — open them from the course dashboard. The untimed ones (6–10) can be split across
sittings: your place is saved. Keep Tests 9 and 10 in reserve for a rework week, or for
after the plan. Afterward, spend as long reviewing as you spent testing:
for each miss, name the reason (content, misreading, trap, or time) and send the misses to
your review queue from the results page. ETS, which makes the GRE, also offers free official
POWERPREP practice tests — the only practice scored by ETS’s own algorithm. Save them for
weeks 7 and 8.
</Concept>

<Example title="Planning around a shorter runway">
Your test is in **4 weeks**, not 8, and you can study about 2½ hours a day. How would you
compress the plan?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Keep the order, double the pace.** Same sequence, twice as fast:

- **Weeks 1–2:** about two lessons a day — one from Math and one from English, so both
  sections move together (the whole course is 29 lessons, about 20 hours in all) — plus
  1 + 1 Foundation sets daily.
- **Week 3:** Core-tier sets, one timed per track daily; drill your three weakest topics.
- **Practice tests:** Test 1 on day 1, Test 3 at the end of week 2, Test 5 early in week
  4 — review each one thoroughly before the next.
- **Week 4:** Advanced-tier timed sets, one official practice test by midweek, and
  your review queue every day. Rest the day before the test.

**Step 2 — Decide what not to cut:** the review queue and timed practice. Those turn “I understood the
lesson” into “I get it right under time pressure,” which is what the test measures.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 3. How to review a missed question

When you miss something, don’t just read the right answer and move on. Ask:

1. **Which choice did I pick, and why was it tempting?** The notes under each choice tell
   you which trap you fell for. Traps repeat — learn their names.
2. **Was it knowledge, method, or carelessness?** Knowledge → reread the lesson (every
   bank question links to the lesson that teaches it). Method → redo a few questions of that
   type from the topic drills. Carelessness → slow down on that step next time.
3. **Can I solve it again cold, tomorrow?** The review queue will check.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — practicing without timing">
Untimed practice teaches methods; only timed practice teaches pacing. Many people who
“know everything” lose points because they never practiced deciding when to move on.
From week 4, at least one set a day should be timed.
</Gotcha>
