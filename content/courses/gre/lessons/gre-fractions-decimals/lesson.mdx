<Intuition>
Fractions are the most common place for careless errors on the GRE — not because the
math is hard, but because there are several procedures and it is easy to apply the wrong
one. The fix is to know exactly what each operation does: you need a common denominator
to **add**, you multiply straight across to **multiply**, and dividing is multiplying by
the reciprocal. Everything else in this lesson is about comparing and interpreting
fractions fast.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## The four operations

- **Add / subtract:** common denominator first. ⅔ + ¾ = 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12.
- **Multiply:** straight across. ⅔ × ¾ = 6/12 = ½. Cancel before multiplying to keep
  numbers small.
- **Divide:** multiply by the reciprocal. ⅔ ÷ ¾ = ⅔ × 4/3 = 8/9.
- **Mixed numbers:** convert first. 2⅓ = 7/3.
</Concept>

<Example title="A compound fraction">
Compute (1/2 + 1/3) ÷ (5/6).

<Solution>
1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. Then 5/6 ÷ 5/6 = **1**.

On the GRE, a numeric-entry question with a fraction answer gives you two boxes
(numerator and denominator). Equivalent fractions are accepted — 10/10 would also be
marked correct — so you don’t have to reduce.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Comparing fractions

- **Cross-multiply:** to compare 5/8 and 7/11, compare 5 × 11 = 55 with 7 × 8 = 56.
  56 is larger, so 7/11 is larger.
- **Benchmarks:** compare each to ½ or 1. 4/9 < ½ < 5/9.
- **Convert to decimals** when the choices mix forms: 3/7 ≈ 0.429, 0.43, (0.65)² ≈ 0.42.

Memorize a few conversions: ⅛ = 0.125, ⅙ ≈ 0.167, ⅕ = 0.2, ¼ = 0.25, ⅓ ≈ 0.333,
⅜ = 0.375, ⅗ = 0.6, ⅔ ≈ 0.667, ¾ = 0.75, ⅞ = 0.875.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Fractions of what’s left

“Spent ⅓ of her money on rent and ¼ **of the remainder** on food.” The second fraction is
of a smaller amount. Track **what remains** at each step: after rent, ⅔ is left; after food,
¾ of that is left, so ⅔ × ¾ = ½ of the original remains.
</Concept>

<Example title="Working backward">
Priya spent 1/4 of her paycheck on rent and then 1/3 of what was left on groceries. She had
$600 left. How much was the paycheck?

<Solution>
After rent, 3/4 remains. After groceries, 2/3 of that remains: 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2 of the
paycheck. So half the paycheck is $600, and the paycheck was **$1,200**.

Trap: treating 1/3 as a fraction of the whole paycheck gives 1 − 1/4 − 1/3 = 5/12
remaining, and 600 ÷ 5/12 = $1,440 — wrong, because the groceries fraction was of the
remainder.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Scientific notation

A number in scientific notation is a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10. Multiply the a’s, add the
exponents, then renormalize:

(4 × 10⁵)(6 × 10³) = 24 × 10⁸ = 2.4 × 10⁹.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — decimals times decimals">
0.3 × 0.3 = 0.09, not 0.9. Count decimal places: each factor has one, so the product has
two. Multiplying two numbers between 0 and 1 always gives something smaller than both.
</Gotcha>
