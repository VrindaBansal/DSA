<Intuition>
Fractions cause more careless mistakes on the GRE than almost anything else — not because
they're hard, but because each operation has its own procedure, and it's easy to use the
wrong one. Adding needs a common denominator; multiplying doesn't. Dividing flips a fraction;
adding doesn't.

This lesson goes through each procedure slowly, says **why** it works, and then practices
the word problems where fractions show up on the test. If you ever get stuck, go back to
the picture of a fraction as a pizza cut into equal slices.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. What a fraction means

A fraction like **3/4** describes **part of a whole**: the whole is cut into **4 equal
pieces** (the bottom number, the **denominator**), and we have **3** of them (the top number,
the **numerator**).

A fraction is also a **division**: 3/4 means 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. That's how you turn any fraction
into a decimal.

**Equivalent fractions.** Multiplying or dividing the top and bottom by the same number
doesn't change the value — it just cuts the pizza into more or fewer slices:
1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 50/100.

**Simplifying (reducing)** means dividing the top and bottom by a number they share, until
they share nothing but 1. For 18/24, both are divisible by 6: 18/24 = 3/4.

**Mixed numbers and improper fractions.** 2⅓ means 2 wholes plus ⅓. To turn it into a single
fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator:
2⅓ = (2 × 3 + 1)/3 = **7/3**. Going the other way, 17/5 = 3 remainder 2, so 17/5 = **3⅖**.
Always convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before doing math with them.
</Concept>

<Example title="Simplify a fraction">
Simplify 84/126.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Find a common factor.** Both are even: 84/126 = 42/63.

**Step 2 — Keep going.** 42 and 63 are both divisible by 7: 42/63 = 6/9.

**Step 3 — Once more.** 6 and 9 are both divisible by 3: 6/9 = 2/3.

**Answer: 2/3.** (Shortcut: the greatest common factor of 84 and 126 is 42, and dividing both by
42 gives 2/3 in one step. But reducing in small steps works just as well.)
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 2. Adding and subtracting fractions

You can only add pieces that are the **same size**. 1/2 + 1/3 isn't 2/5 — halves and thirds are
different-sized slices. So first, rewrite both fractions with the **same denominator**, then add
the numerators and keep the denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Find a **common denominator** — ideally the least common multiple (LCM) of the denominators.
2. Rewrite each fraction with that denominator (multiply top and bottom by the same number).
3. Add or subtract the **numerators only**. The denominator stays the same.
4. Simplify if you can.

$$
\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}
$$
</Concept>

<Example title="Add with the least common denominator">
Compute 5/6 + 3/8.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Common denominator.** LCM of 6 and 8: multiples of 8 are 8, 16, **24**; 24 is also a
multiple of 6. Use 24.

**Step 2 — Rewrite.** 5/6 = 20/24 (multiply top and bottom by 4). 3/8 = 9/24 (multiply by 3).

**Step 3 — Add the numerators.** 20/24 + 9/24 = 29/24.

**Answer: 29/24**, which is 1 5/24 as a mixed number.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Example title="Subtract mixed numbers">
Compute 4⅓ − 2⅚.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Convert to improper fractions.** 4⅓ = 13/3 and 2⅚ = 17/6.

**Step 2 — Common denominator (6).** 13/3 = 26/6.

**Step 3 — Subtract.** 26/6 − 17/6 = 9/6.

**Step 4 — Simplify.** 9/6 = 3/2 = **1½**.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 3. Multiplying and dividing fractions

**Multiplying is easier than adding:** multiply straight across, top × top and bottom × bottom.
No common denominator needed.

$$
\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

**Cancel first** to keep the numbers small: any top number can cancel with any bottom number.
In 2/3 × 3/4, the 3s cancel and the 2 cancels into the 4, leaving 1/2 directly.

The word **"of" means multiply**: "2/3 of 90" is 2/3 × 90 = 60.

**Dividing: flip the second fraction, then multiply.** "Keep, change, flip":

$$
\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{3}{4} = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{9}
$$

Why does flipping work? Dividing asks "how many of these fit?" How many ½-cup scoops fit in 3
cups? Each cup holds 2 scoops, so 6 — and that's 3 ÷ ½ = 3 × 2 = 6.
</Concept>

<Example title="Cancel before multiplying">
Compute 14/15 × 25/28.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Cancel across.** 14 (top) and 28 (bottom) share 14: they become 1 and 2.
25 (top) and 15 (bottom) share 5: they become 5 and 3.

**Step 2 — Multiply what's left.** (1 × 5)/(3 × 2) = **5/6**.

Without canceling you'd compute 350/420 and then have to reduce it — same answer, more work
and more chances for errors.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Example title="A fraction inside a fraction">
Compute (1/2 + 1/3) ÷ (5/6).

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Do the top first (parentheses).** 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

**Step 2 — Divide.** 5/6 ÷ 5/6 = 5/6 × 6/5 = **1**. (Any nonzero number divided by itself is 1.)

GRE note: when a numeric-entry answer is a fraction, you get two boxes (top and bottom), and
equivalent fractions are accepted, so you don't have to reduce.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 4. Comparing fractions

Three ways, from quickest to most reliable:

**Benchmarks.** Compare each fraction to ½ or to 1. Is the top more or less than half the
bottom? 4/9 is less than ½ (4 is less than 4.5); 5/9 is more. So 4/9 < 5/9 without any math.

**Cross-multiplying.** To compare a/b with c/d, compare **a × d** with **c × b**. For 5/8 vs. 7/11:
5 × 11 = 55 and 7 × 8 = 56. The bigger product belongs to the fraction on its top, so
**7/11 is bigger**. (This works because it's the same as giving both fractions the denominator 88.)

**Convert to decimals** when the choices mix fractions, decimals, and roots. These are worth
memorizing:

| fraction | 1/8 | 1/6 | 1/5 | 1/4 | 1/3 | 3/8 | 2/5 | 1/2 | 3/5 | 5/8 | 2/3 | 3/4 | 4/5 | 7/8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decimal | .125 | .167 | .2 | .25 | .333 | .375 | .4 | .5 | .6 | .625 | .667 | .75 | .8 | .875 |
</Concept>

<Example title="Which is bigger?">
Which is greater, 7/12 or 4/7?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Cross-multiply.** 7/12 vs 4/7: 7 × 7 = 49 (goes with 7/12) and 4 × 12 = 48 (goes with 4/7).

**Step 2 — Compare.** 49 > 48, so **7/12 is greater** — just barely.

**Check with decimals:** 7/12 ≈ 0.583 and 4/7 ≈ 0.571. ✓ Benchmarks wouldn't settle this one:
both are just over ½.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 5. Decimals

**Converting.** A decimal is a fraction with a denominator of 10, 100, 1,000…:
0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20. And a fraction becomes a decimal by dividing: 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

**Multiplying decimals.** Ignore the decimal points, multiply the whole numbers, then put the
point back: the answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers **together**.
0.3 × 0.02: 3 × 2 = 6, and there are 1 + 2 = 3 decimal places, so the answer is **0.006**.

**Dividing decimals.** Move the decimal point in **both** numbers the same number of places until
you're dividing by a whole number. 2.4 ÷ 0.06: move both points two places → 240 ÷ 6 = **40**.
(This is the same as multiplying top and bottom by 100, which doesn't change the value.)

**Place value** matters for rounding: in 3.1416, the 1 is in the tenths place, the 4 in the
hundredths, the 1 in the thousandths. Rounded to the nearest hundredth, 3.1416 is 3.14.
</Concept>

<Gotcha title="Trap — decimals times decimals">
0.3 × 0.3 = 0.09, not 0.9. Count decimal places: each factor has one, so the product has
two. Multiplying two numbers between 0 and 1 always gives something **smaller than both** —
if your answer got bigger, a decimal point slipped.
</Gotcha>

<Example title="Divide by a decimal">
What is 0.45 ÷ 0.015?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Make the divisor a whole number.** 0.015 has three decimal places, so move both
points three places right: 0.45 → 450 and 0.015 → 15.

**Step 2 — Divide.** 450 ÷ 15 = **30**.

**Check:** 30 × 0.015 = 0.45 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 6. Fractions of what's left

Word problems love this phrase: "She spent 1/3 of her money on rent and **1/4 of the
remainder** on food." The second fraction is a fraction of a **smaller amount** — what was
left after rent — not of the original.

**The reliable method: track what remains after each step.**
- After spending 1/3, **2/3** remains.
- After spending 1/4 of that, **3/4 of the 2/3** remains: 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2 of the original.

Multiply the "what's left" fractions together. If you know how much is left at the end, divide
by that fraction to get the original amount.

Another option that always works: **pick a convenient starting number** — one divisible by all
the denominators (here, 12) — and follow the dollars.
</Concept>

<Example title="Working backward to the start">
Priya spent 1/4 of her paycheck on rent and then 1/3 of what was left on groceries. She had
$600 left. How much was the paycheck?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — After rent,** 1 − 1/4 = 3/4 of the paycheck remains.

**Step 2 — After groceries,** she keeps 1 − 1/3 = 2/3 of that: 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12 = 1/2 of the paycheck.

**Step 3 — Solve.** Half the paycheck is $600, so the paycheck was **$1,200**.

**Check by following the money:** $1,200 − $300 rent = $900; 1/3 of $900 = $300 groceries; $900 − $300 = $600 ✓.

**The trap:** treating 1/3 as a fraction of the whole paycheck: 1 − 1/4 − 1/3 = 5/12 left, which
gives $1,440 — wrong, because groceries were 1/3 of the *remainder*.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 7. Scientific notation

Scientific notation writes very big or very small numbers as **(a number from 1 to just under 10)
× (a power of 10)**:

- 45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴ (move the decimal point 4 places left)
- 0.0032 = 3.2 × 10⁻³ (move it 3 places right; small numbers get negative exponents)

**To multiply:** multiply the front numbers, add the exponents, then fix the front number if it
isn't between 1 and 10.

(4 × 10⁵) × (6 × 10³) = 24 × 10⁸ = **2.4 × 10⁹** (24 = 2.4 × 10, so one more power of 10).

**To divide:** divide the front numbers and **subtract** the exponents.
</Concept>

<Example title="Divide in scientific notation">
Compute (9 × 10⁴) ÷ (3 × 10⁻²).

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Divide the front numbers.** 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

**Step 2 — Subtract the exponents.** 10⁴ ÷ 10⁻² = 10^(4 − (−2)) = 10⁶. (Subtracting a negative adds.)

**Answer: 3 × 10⁶**, which is 3,000,000.
</Solution>
</Example>

## Recap

| Operation | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| simplify | divide top and bottom by a shared factor | 18/24 = 3/4 |
| mixed → improper | whole × bottom + top, over bottom | 2⅓ = 7/3 |
| add / subtract | common denominator, then add tops | 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 |
| multiply | straight across; cancel first; "of" = × | 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2 |
| divide | flip the second fraction and multiply | 2/3 ÷ 3/4 = 8/9 |
| compare | benchmarks, cross-multiply, or decimals | 5/8 < 7/11 (55 < 56) |
| decimal × decimal | count total decimal places | 0.3 × 0.02 = 0.006 |
| ÷ by a decimal | shift both points until the divisor is whole | 2.4 ÷ 0.06 = 240 ÷ 6 |
| fraction of the rest | multiply the "what's left" fractions | ⅔ × ¾ = ½ left |
| scientific notation | multiply fronts, add exponents, then fix the front | 24 × 10⁸ = 2.4 × 10⁹ |
</Concept>
