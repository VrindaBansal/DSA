<Intuition>
Multi-blank completions look scary, but they give you something one-blank questions don't: **every
blank you solve becomes a clue for the others.**

The skill is choosing **where to start**. Blank (i) is often the hardest, because it comes before most
of the clues. So find the blank the sentence pins down most tightly, solve it, and let it unlock the
rest. Finally — because there's **no partial credit** — reread the whole finished sentence to make sure
it hangs together.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. The format and the method

Two- and three-blank questions give **three choices per blank**, in separate columns. You pick one word
from each column. The question only counts as right if **every** blank is right.

**The method:**
1. Read the whole sentence first.
2. **Start with the easiest blank** — the one with the most direct clue. It can be any of them.
3. Predict it, match it, and treat your answer as a fixed part of the sentence.
4. Move to the next blank, using everything you now know.
5. **Reread the complete sentence.** If it doesn't make sense as a whole, one blank is wrong.
</Concept>

<Example title="Start where the clue is">
"The committee's chair, usually (i)___ in debate, astonished her colleagues by losing her temper; her
outburst, however, did not (ii)___ them, since they agreed her objections were entirely (iii)___."

| Blank (i) | Blank (ii) | Blank (iii) |
|---|---|---|
| imperturbable | provoke | irrelevant |
| volatile | placate | pertinent |
| taciturn | confirm | frivolous |

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Blank (i) has a strong clue.** She "astonished her colleagues by losing her temper," so
losing her temper is unusual for her. Usually she's calm: **imperturbable** (hard to upset).
*Volatile* is the trap — it describes the outburst, not her usual self. *Taciturn* is about talking,
not temper.

**Step 2 — Blank (iii) next.** Her colleagues "agreed" with her objections, so the objections were
relevant: **pertinent**.

**Step 3 — Blank (ii) last.** Since they agreed with her, the outburst didn't anger them: **provoke**.
*Placate* (calm down) reverses the logic.

**Step 4 — Reread.** "Usually imperturbable … did not provoke them, since … entirely pertinent." ✓
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## 2. Sentence structures that tell you the answer

A few structures show up again and again. Spotting them tells you how the blanks relate **before** you
look at a single choice:

| Structure | What it means for the blanks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| "once X, now Y" / "no longer X, but Y" | X and Y are **opposites** | once praised as *concise*, now criticized as *wordy* |
| "not X but Y" | X and Y are **opposites**, and the clue supports Y | not *flattering* but *balanced* |
| "because X, Y" / "X, so Y" | X and Y go the **same direction** | because *careless*, the report was *riddled with errors* |
| "tried to X, but Y happened anyway" | X is an attempt that **failed** | tried to *suppress* the news, but it *spread* |

**Use them in both directions:** once you've solved one blank, the structure tells you what the other
must be.
</Concept>

<Example title="Once X, now Y">
"The once (i)___ town, whose mills employed thousands, is now (ii)___: shuttered storefronts line its
main street, and young people leave as soon as they can."

| Blank (i) | Blank (ii) |
|---|---|
| prosperous | bustling |
| desolate | moribund |
| tranquil | picturesque |

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Spot the structure.** "Once (i) … now (ii)" — the blanks are **opposites**.

**Step 2 — Blank (ii) has the clue.** "Shuttered storefronts … young people leave" — the town is
dying: **moribund** (close to death, declining).

**Step 3 — Blank (i) is the opposite.** Mills employing thousands — thriving: **prosperous**.

**Step 4 — Reread.** "The once prosperous town … is now moribund: shuttered storefronts…" ✓

**Traps:** *desolate* (empty, bleak) describes the **now**, not the "once." *Bustling* describes the
past, not the present.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Gotcha title="Trap — solving blank (i) on its own">
Blank (i)'s choices often include a word **and its opposite**, and both can look fine until you read the
rest of the sentence. If blank (i) feels like a coin flip, skip it, solve a later blank, and come back.
</Gotcha>

<Concept>
## Recap

| Step | Why |
|---|---|
| read the whole sentence | the clues are often after blank (i) |
| start with the strongest clue | a solid first blank makes the rest easier |
| use the structure | "once X, now Y" → opposites; "because X, Y" → same direction |
| solved blank = new clue | each answer narrows the others |
| reread at the end | no partial credit — the whole sentence must work |
</Concept>
