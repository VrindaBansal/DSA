<Intuition>
Most GRE statistics questions are about two ideas: **where the data sit** (mean, median)
and **how spread out they are** (range, standard deviation). You almost never compute a
standard deviation by hand — you compare spreads. And average problems become easy the
moment you switch from averages to **sums**, because sums add and averages don’t.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Averages: think in sums

**Sum = average × count.** When numbers are added, removed, or combined, work with sums.
</Concept>

<Example title="Adding a number">
The average of 5 numbers is 12. When a sixth number is added, the average becomes 14. What
is the sixth number?

<Solution>
Old sum = 5 × 12 = 60. New sum = 6 × 14 = 84. Sixth number = 84 − 60 = **24**.

Intuition check: it must be 14 (the new average) plus 2 extra for each of the 5 old
numbers it raises: 14 + 10 = 24 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Weighted averages

Combining groups of different sizes: combined average = (total of all values) ÷ (total
count). The result lies between the group averages, **closer to the larger group’s**.

20 students averaging 80 and 30 averaging 90: (1,600 + 2,700) ÷ 50 = 86 — not 85.
</Concept>

<Concept>
## Median, mode, range, quartiles

- **Median:** sort, then take the middle value (or the average of the two middle values).
- **Mode:** the most frequent value. **Range:** max − min.
- **Quartiles:** Q1 is the median of the lower half, Q3 of the upper half.
  **Interquartile range** = Q3 − Q1: the range of the middle half of the data.
- Outliers pull the **mean** toward them; the median barely moves.
</Concept>

<Check id="gre-stat-median" />

<Concept>
## Standard deviation is spread

Standard deviation (SD) measures how far values typically sit from the mean.

- All values equal → SD = 0.
- **Add** the same number to every value → the SD **doesn’t change** (everything shifts
  together).
- **Multiply** every value by k → the SD is multiplied by |k|.
- To compare SDs, compare how spread out the values are relative to their own mean — not
  how big they are. The lists 1, 2, 3 and 101, 102, 103 have the same SD.
</Concept>

<Check id="gre-stat-sd" />

<Concept>
## The normal distribution

For data that are approximately normal (a symmetric bell curve):

- about **68%** fall within 1 SD of the mean,
- about **95%** within 2 SDs,
- about **99.7%** within 3 SDs.

Split into regions: 34% between the mean and 1 SD (each side), 13.5% between 1 and 2 SDs,
and 2.5% beyond 2 SDs (each side). So about 16% are above +1 SD, and about 84% are below it.
</Concept>

<Example title="Normal distribution">
Test scores are normally distributed with mean 500 and SD 100. About what percent of
scores are between 400 and 700?

<Solution>
400 is −1 SD; 700 is +2 SD. From −1 to the mean: 34%. From the mean to +2: 34% + 13.5% = 47.5%.
Total ≈ **81.5%**.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Gotcha title="Trap — averaging averages">
The average of two group averages equals the overall average only if the groups are the
same size. Otherwise, weight by the counts.
</Gotcha>
