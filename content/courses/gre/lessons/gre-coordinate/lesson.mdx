<Intuition>
Coordinate geometry is where algebra and geometry meet, and the GRE keeps it simple:
lines, their slopes, and the distances between points. Distance is just the Pythagorean
theorem in disguise, and slope is “rise over run.” Sketching a quick graph on your scratch
paper catches most sign errors.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## Two points

For (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):

- **Slope** = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁) — rise over run. Subtract in the same order on top and
  bottom.
- **Midpoint** = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2) — average the coordinates.
- **Distance** = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) — the hypotenuse of the right triangle whose
  legs are the horizontal and vertical gaps.
</Concept>

<Example title="Slope and distance">
Find the slope and the distance between (−2, 1) and (4, 9).

<Solution>
Slope = (9 − 1)/(4 − (−2)) = 8/6 = **4/3**.
Distance = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = **10** (a 6-8-10 triangle).
</Solution>
</Example>

<Concept>
## Lines

**Slope-intercept form:** y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b the y-intercept.

- **x-intercept:** set y = 0. **y-intercept:** set x = 0.
- **Parallel lines** have equal slopes. **Perpendicular lines** have slopes that are
  negative reciprocals (their product is −1): 2/3 and −3/2.
- A point lies on a line exactly when its coordinates satisfy the equation.
- Horizontal lines (y = k) have slope 0; vertical lines (x = k) have undefined slope.
</Concept>

<Example title="Line through two points">
Find the equation of the line through (1, 5) and (3, 11).

<Solution>
Slope = (11 − 5)/(3 − 1) = 3. Then y = 3x + b; plug in (1, 5): 5 = 3 + b → b = 2.
**y = 3x + 2.**
</Solution>
</Example>

<Gotcha title="Trap — intercept confusion">
For y = 2x − 6, the y-intercept is −6 but the x-intercept is 3 (set y = 0: 2x = 6).
Questions often ask for one and offer the other as a choice.
</Gotcha>
