<Intuition>
The coordinate plane is a map. Every point has an address **(x, y)**: x says how far **left or right**
of the center, and y says how far **up or down**. Once points have addresses, geometry turns into
arithmetic:

- the steepness of a line is **rise over run**,
- the distance between two points is the **Pythagorean theorem**,
- and a point is on a graph when its numbers **make the equation true**.

A quick sketch on scratch paper catches most mistakes, especially sign errors.
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. The plane, quadrants, and reflections

The two axes split the plane into four **quadrants**, numbered counter-clockwise starting from the top
right. The signs of x and y tell you which one a point is in:

**Reflections** just flip signs:
- across the **x-axis** (flip up/down): (x, y) → (x, **−y**)
- across the **y-axis** (flip left/right): (x, y) → (**−x**, y)
- across the line **y = x**: swap them, (x, y) → (**y, x**)

**Letters instead of numbers?** If you're told (a, b) is in quadrant II, translate: a is negative and
b is positive. Then work out the signs of whatever new point they ask about.
</Concept>

<Example title="Signs with letters">
The point (a, b) is in quadrant III. Which quadrant is the point (−a, b) in?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Translate the quadrant.** Quadrant III is (−, −), so a is negative and b is negative.

**Step 2 — Work out each coordinate.** −a is the negative of a negative, so it's **positive**. b is
still **negative**.

**Step 3 — Read the quadrant.** (+, −) is **quadrant IV**.

**Plug-in check:** pick (a, b) = (−2, −3). Then (−a, b) = (2, −3), which is in quadrant IV ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 2. Slope: rise over run

**Slope** measures steepness: how much the line goes **up** (rise) for each step to the **right**
(run).

$$
\text{slope} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

**Subtract in the same order on top and bottom** — second point minus first point for both.

What the slope tells you:

| Slope | The line… |
|---|---|
| positive | goes **up** from left to right |
| negative | goes **down** from left to right |
| 0 | is flat (horizontal), like y = 4 |
| undefined | is straight up and down (vertical), like x = 2 |

A bigger slope (ignoring the sign) means a **steeper** line: slope 3 is steeper than slope ½.
</Concept>

<Example title="Slope between two points">
What is the slope of the line through (−2, 1) and (4, 9)?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Rise (change in y).** 9 − 1 = 8.

**Step 2 — Run (change in x), same order.** 4 − (−2) = 6.

**Step 3 — Divide.** 8/6 = **4/3**. Positive, so the line rises from left to right ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 3. Distance and midpoint

**Distance** between two points is the Pythagorean theorem. Draw the right triangle: one leg is the
horizontal gap, the other is the vertical gap, and the distance is the hypotenuse.

<svg viewBox="0 0 260 215" width="100%" style={{maxWidth: '260px'}} role="img" aria-label="Points (−2, 1) and (4, 9) joined by a segment of length 10, forming a right triangle with a horizontal leg of 6 and a vertical leg of 8.">

  <polyline points="150,182 150,170 162,170" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeWidth="1" />

  <text x="170" y="36" fontSize="12" fill="currentColor">(4, 9)</text>
  <text x="108" y="200" fontSize="13" fill="currentColor" textAnchor="middle">6</text>
  <text x="170" y="114" fontSize="13" fill="currentColor">8</text>
  <text x="98" y="104" fontSize="13" fill="currentColor" textAnchor="end">10</text>
</svg>

$$
\text{distance} = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
$$

You don't need to memorize that — just find the two gaps and use a² + b² = c² (and look for a triple:
the gaps 6 and 8 above make a 6-8-10 triangle).

**Midpoint** — the point exactly halfway between. **Average the x's and average the y's:**

$$
\text{midpoint} = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)
$$

The midpoint of (−2, 1) and (4, 9) is ((−2 + 4)/2, (1 + 9)/2) = **(1, 5)**.

**Going backward** (you know one endpoint and the midpoint): the midpoint is halfway, so take the same
step again. From (−2, 1) to the midpoint (1, 5) is "right 3, up 4"; do it again to reach (4, 9).
</Concept>

<Example title="Distance between two points">
What is the distance between (1, 3) and (−5, 11)?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Horizontal gap.** From x = 1 to x = −5 is 6 units. (Sign doesn't matter — it gets squared.)

**Step 2 — Vertical gap.** From y = 3 to y = 11 is 8 units.

**Step 3 — Hypotenuse.** √(6² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = **10**.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 4. The equation of a line

Most GRE lines are written in **slope-intercept form**:

$$
y = mx + b
$$

- **m** is the slope.
- **b** is the **y-intercept** — where the line crosses the y-axis (the point (0, b)).

**To find the equation from two points:** find the slope m, then plug either point into y = mx + b and
solve for b.

**Intercepts:**
- **y-intercept:** set **x = 0** and solve for y.
- **x-intercept:** set **y = 0** and solve for x.

**Is a point on the line?** Plug it in. If the equation comes out true, the point is on the line.

If the line is written some other way, like 2x + 3y = 12, solve for y to get it into y = mx + b form:
3y = −2x + 12, so y = −(2/3)x + 4.
</Concept>

<Example title="Line through two points">
Find the equation of the line through (1, 5) and (3, 11). Then find its x-intercept.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Slope.** (11 − 5)/(3 − 1) = 6/2 = 3. So y = 3x + b.

**Step 2 — Find b with either point.** Use (1, 5): 5 = 3(1) + b, so b = 2. The line is **y = 3x + 2**.

**Step 3 — Check with the other point.** 3(3) + 2 = 11 ✓.

**Step 4 — x-intercept: set y = 0.** 0 = 3x + 2, so x = **−2/3**.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Gotcha title="Trap — mixing up the intercepts">
For y = 2x − 6, the **y**-intercept is −6 but the **x**-intercept is 3 (set y = 0: 2x = 6). Questions
often ask for one and offer the other as an answer choice.
</Gotcha>

## 5. Parallel and perpendicular lines

- **Parallel** lines have the **same slope**. (Same steepness, so they never meet.)
- **Perpendicular** lines (crossing at a right angle) have slopes that are **negative reciprocals**:
  flip the fraction **and** change the sign.

| Slope | Parallel slope | Perpendicular slope |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 | −1/2 |
| −3/4 | −3/4 | 4/3 |
| 1/5 | 1/5 | −5 |

A quick check for perpendicular: the two slopes **multiply to −1**. (2 × −½ = −1 ✓)
</Concept>

<Example title="A perpendicular line through a point">
Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = 2x + 1 that passes through (4, 3).

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Perpendicular slope.** The given slope is 2 (that is, 2/1). Flip and change the sign: −1/2.

**Step 2 — Find b.** y = −½x + b. Plug in (4, 3): 3 = −½(4) + b = −2 + b, so b = 5.

**Answer: y = −½x + 5.** Check: −½(4) + 5 = 3 ✓, and 2 × (−½) = −1 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 6. Where graphs cross

A point where two graphs **cross** is on **both** graphs, so its coordinates make **both** equations
true. To find it, **solve the equations together** — usually by setting the two y's equal.

The same idea finds where a graph crosses an **axis**: the x-axis is where y = 0, and the y-axis is
where x = 0. For a curve like y = x² − 9, setting y = 0 gives x² = 9, so it crosses the x-axis at
x = 3 and x = −3.
</Concept>

<Example title="Two lines meet">
Where do the lines y = 3x − 2 and y = −x + 6 intersect?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Set the y's equal.** 3x − 2 = −x + 6.

**Step 2 — Solve for x.** 4x = 8, so x = 2.

**Step 3 — Find y with either equation.** y = 3(2) − 2 = 4. Check with the other: −2 + 6 = 4 ✓.

**Answer: (2, 4).**
</Solution>
</Example>

## Recap

| Tool | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quadrants | I (+, +), II (−, +), III (−, −), IV (+, −) | (−2, −3) is in III |
| reflect | x-axis: flip y's sign; y-axis: flip x's sign | (3, 5) → (3, −5) across the x-axis |
| slope | rise ÷ run, same order top and bottom | (−2, 1) to (4, 9): 8/6 = 4/3 |
| distance | the two gaps, then a² + b² = c² | gaps 6 and 8 → 10 |
| midpoint | average the x's, average the y's | (−2, 1) and (4, 9) → (1, 5) |
| line | y = mx + b; find m, then plug in a point for b | through (1, 5), (3, 11): y = 3x + 2 |
| intercepts | x-intercept: y = 0; y-intercept: x = 0 | y = 2x − 6 → x-int 3, y-int −6 |
| parallel / perpendicular | same slope / flip and change sign | 2 → 2 / −½ |
| crossing point | solve the equations together | y = 3x − 2, y = −x + 6 → (2, 4) |
</Concept>
