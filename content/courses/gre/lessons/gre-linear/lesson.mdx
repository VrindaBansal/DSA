<Intuition>
An equation is a **balance**: the left side weighs exactly what the right side weighs. You can do
anything you like to it — add, subtract, multiply, divide — **as long as you do the same thing to
both sides**. Solving just means doing those moves until the unknown is alone.

Everything in this lesson builds on that one idea. The only new wrinkles are inequalities (where
one move flips the sign) and absolute value (where there are usually two answers).
</Intuition>

<Concept>
## 1. Solving one-variable equations

The goal is to get x alone on one side. A reliable order:

1. **Clear parentheses** by distributing: 3(x − 4) = 3x − 12.
2. **Clear fractions** by multiplying every term by the least common denominator.
3. **Collect** the x-terms on one side and the plain numbers on the other.
4. **Divide** by the number in front of x.
5. **Check** by plugging your answer back in — it takes ten seconds and catches most slips.
</Concept>

<Example title="Parentheses on both sides">
Solve 3(x − 4) + 5 = 2x + 7.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Distribute.** 3x − 12 + 5 = 2x + 7, so 3x − 7 = 2x + 7.

**Step 2 — Collect x on the left.** Subtract 2x from both sides: x − 7 = 7.

**Step 3 — Isolate x.** Add 7 to both sides: **x = 14**.

**Step 4 — Check.** Left: 3(10) + 5 = 35. Right: 28 + 7 = 35 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Example title="An equation with fractions">
Solve x/3 + x/4 = 14.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Clear the fractions.** The least common denominator of 3 and 4 is 12. Multiply every
term by 12: 12 · (x/3) + 12 · (x/4) = 12 · 14, which is 4x + 3x = 168.

**Step 2 — Combine and divide.** 7x = 168, so **x = 24**.

**Check:** 24/3 + 24/4 = 8 + 6 = 14 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 2. Two equations, two unknowns

With two unknowns you need two equations. There are two standard methods:

**Elimination** — add or subtract the equations so one variable cancels.
x + y = 10 and x − y = 4. Add them: 2x = 14, so x = 7, and then y = 3.

**Substitution** — solve one equation for a variable and plug it into the other.
y = 2x − 1 and 3x + y = 14 → 3x + (2x − 1) = 14 → 5x = 15 → x = 3, y = 5.

**But first, look for a shortcut.** The GRE often asks for a **combination** like x + y instead of
x and y separately. Adding or subtracting the equations sometimes produces it directly:
3x + 2y = 17 and 2x + 3y = 13. Add them: 5x + 5y = 30, so **x + y = 6** — no need to find x or y.
</Concept>

<Example title="Elimination with multiplying">
Solve 2x + 3y = 12 and 5x − 2y = 11.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Make one variable cancel.** Multiply the first equation by 2 and the second by 3 so the
y-terms become +6y and −6y:
4x + 6y = 24 and 15x − 6y = 33.

**Step 2 — Add.** 19x = 57, so x = 3.

**Step 3 — Substitute back.** 2(3) + 3y = 12 → 3y = 6 → y = 2.

**Answer: x = 3, y = 2.** Check in the second equation: 15 − 4 = 11 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 3. Inequalities

Solve an inequality exactly like an equation, with **one extra rule**:

**When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, flip the inequality sign.**

Why? 2 < 3 is true. Multiply both sides by −1: −2 and −3. Now −2 is the *bigger* one, so the sign
must flip: −2 > −3.

The answer to an inequality is a **range** of values, like x < −4. When a question asks "how many
integers" satisfy it, list them, and watch whether the ends are included (≤ and ≥ include them; < and > don't).

A **double inequality** like 3 < 2x + 1 ≤ 15 is solved by doing the same thing to all three parts.
</Concept>

<Example title="Dividing by a negative">
Solve 5 − 3x > 17.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Subtract 5 from both sides.** −3x > 12.

**Step 2 — Divide by −3, and flip the sign.** x < −4.

**Check with a number in the range**, like x = −5: 5 − 3(−5) = 20, and 20 > 17 ✓. A number outside it,
like x = 0, gives 5 > 17 ✗. Good.
</Solution>
</Example>

<Example title="A double inequality">
How many integers x satisfy −7 ≤ 2x − 3 < 5?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Add 3 to all three parts.** −4 ≤ 2x < 8.

**Step 2 — Divide all three parts by 2** (positive, so no flip). −2 ≤ x < 4.

**Step 3 — List the integers.** −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3. (−2 is included because of the ≤ sign; 4 is not, because of the < sign.)

**Answer: 6 integers.**
</Solution>
</Example>

## 4. Absolute value is distance

**|x| is the distance from x to 0** on the number line, so it's never negative: |5| = 5 and |−5| = 5.

More generally, **|x − a| is the distance between x and a.** That picture makes absolute-value
questions easy:

- **|x − 3| = 5** means "x is 5 away from 3": x = 8 or x = −2. **Two answers.**
- **|x − 3| < 5** means "x is less than 5 away from 3": −2 < x < 8. **A range between.**
- **|x − 3| > 5** means "more than 5 away": x < −2 or x > 8. **Two outer ranges.**

**The algebra version** of |expression| = k: write two equations, expression = k and
expression = −k, and solve both.
</Concept>

<Example title="Two cases">
Solve |2x + 1| = 9.

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Split into two cases.** 2x + 1 = 9 or 2x + 1 = −9.

**Step 2 — Solve each.** First: 2x = 8, x = 4. Second: 2x = −10, x = −5.

**Answer: x = 4 or x = −5.** Check: |9| = 9 and |−9| = 9 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## 5. Turning words into equations

Word problems are mostly translation. Name the unknown, then turn each phrase into math:

| words | math |
|---|---|
| a number, the amount | x (say what x stands for) |
| is, was, equals, will be | = |
| more than, increased by, sum | + |
| less than, decreased by, difference | − |
| times, of, product | × |
| twice, double | 2 × |

**Watch the order with "less than."** "5 less than x" means start with x and take 5 away: **x − 5**,
not 5 − x. Same with "more than": "7 more than twice n" is 2n + 7.

When a problem has two unknowns (like adult and child tickets), write **one equation for each fact**
— usually one for the count and one for the money.
</Concept>

<Example title="Two facts, two equations">
A jar holds 40 coins, all nickels and dimes, worth $3.10 in total. How many dimes are in the jar?

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Name the unknowns.** n = nickels, d = dimes.

**Step 2 — One equation per fact.** Count: n + d = 40. Value, in cents: 5n + 10d = 310.

**Step 3 — Substitute.** n = 40 − d, so 5(40 − d) + 10d = 310 → 200 + 5d = 310 → 5d = 110 → **d = 22**.

**Check:** 18 nickels (90¢) + 22 dimes ($2.20) = $3.10 ✓.
</Solution>
</Example>

## Recap

| Situation | Do this | Example |
|---|---|---|
| one equation | distribute, clear fractions, collect, divide, check | x/3 + x/4 = 14 → x = 24 |
| two equations | eliminate or substitute | x + y = 10, x − y = 4 → x = 7 |
| asked for x + y | add or subtract the equations first | 5x + 5y = 30 → x + y = 6 |
| inequality | like an equation, but flip when × or ÷ by a negative | −3x > 12 → x < −4 |
| \|x − a\| = k | two answers, a ± k | \|x − 3\| = 5 → 8 or −2 |
| \|x − a\| < k | within k of a | −2 < x < 8 |
| "5 less than x" | x − 5 | not 5 − x |
</Concept>
